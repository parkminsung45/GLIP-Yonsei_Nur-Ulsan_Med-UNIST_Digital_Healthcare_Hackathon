# GLIP (Yonsei UNIV. Nursing - Ulsan Univ. Med - UNIST Digital Healthcare Hackathon

> **While you're on it, and after you stop.**  
> An avatar-based self-care concept for people using GLP-1 medications.

**GLIP** is a digital-health hackathon project that explores how people using GLP-1 medications can receive continuous, human-centered self-care support - both during treatment and after discontinuation. Rather than treating health tracking as a score to optimize, GLIP uses an avatar as a mirror of the user's condition and connects concerning patterns back to clinical care when needed.

## The idea

GLP-1 medications can support meaningful weight reduction, but users may face a care gap in two moments: when adverse symptoms are easy to dismiss during treatment, and when appetite and weight regain become difficult to manage after stopping medication.

GLIP proposes a care journey that adapts to these changing needs:

```text
Start treatment → symptom self-checks → safety guidance
       ↓
Stop medication → lifestyle and weight-regain monitoring → clinic reconnection when needed
```

The goal is not to replace clinicians. It is to help users notice their own condition earlier, maintain honest records, and arrive at the right care at the right time.

## Proposed experience

### During treatment: notice safety signals

- A one-minute, category-first symptom check reduces the effort of daily logging.
- An avatar reflects the user's reported condition on a five-level scale. It is a **health mirror**, not a reward system.
- High-risk signs trigger an immediate safety notice and guidance to seek appropriate medical care, independent of the avatar score.
- With consent, a concise four-week report can summarize symptom trends, persistent symptoms, dose changes, and the worst recorded day for a clinic visit.

### After discontinuation: catch regain early

- Optional meal photos are tagged for protein, vegetables, and refined carbohydrates to make dietary patterns easier to see.
- Weekly weight trends are translated into a green / yellow / red traffic-light signal.
- A yellow signal prompts an early, structured reset; a red signal recommends reconnecting with a clinic and sharing the report.
- A 52-week, four-stage maintenance journey is designed to support the transition after medication stops.

### A deliberate incentive design

GLIP separates condition from effort. Symptoms or weight changes affect the avatar's state, while honest logging earns the same in-app "sprouts" regardless of whether the day was good or difficult. This is intended to discourage users from hiding concerning symptoms simply to preserve a positive score.

## Key product principles

| Principle | How GLIP applies it |
| --- | --- |
| Self-care, not self-blame | The avatar makes condition changes visible without framing health as a streak or a competition. |
| Journey-adaptive care | Support changes between medication use and discontinuation rather than ending at a prescription reminder. |
| Safety first | Potential red flags bypass gamification and point users toward medical help. |
| Clinic-ready continuity | Consent-based summaries help clinicians quickly understand what happened between visits. |
| Privacy by design | Any future clinical reporting or analytics requires explicit user consent and appropriate de-identification. |

## Project scope

This repository documents a hackathon concept, product design, user journeys, market research, and validation plan. It is **not** a medical device, a diagnostic tool, or clinical advice. The proposed symptom rules, thresholds, and clinical workflow would require review by qualified clinicians, user research, privacy and security design, and regulatory assessment before real-world use.

## Validation roadmap

1. **MVP and user validation (0-3 months):** test check-in retention, reporting completion, and response to safety guidance.
2. **Clinic pilot (3-6 months):** evaluate four-week reports and clinician workflow with 5-10 clinics.
3. **Paid conversion (6-12 months):** validate willingness to pay, sustained use, and clinic adoption.
4. **Data-informed expansion (12-36 months):** study an evidence-based regain-risk model only after sufficient longitudinal data and validation.
